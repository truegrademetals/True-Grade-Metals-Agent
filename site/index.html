Five questions you can’t answer from Brisbane.

Buying superalloy direct from China can take a serious slice out of your material cost. It also hands you a set of unknowns that no amount of email can close, which is why most Australian buyers who could do it, don’t.

  1. Is this mill real?
  2. Is the grade real?
  3. Is the mill test certificate real?
  4. Will it actually ship?
  5. And when it’s wrong, who do I call?

The last one has no workaround. Every other question has a hack: a reference, a sample, a smaller first order. That one is just exposure, and it is the reason the container never gets booked. We answer it by being the party you call.

If it isn’t the grade you specified, it doesn’t ship.

The heat is tested by an accredited independent laboratory before it leaves China, and the report reaches you unedited. If it does not meet what was specified, the container does not load — and where something gets past us, the material is replaced.

That is the whole reason to put someone between you and the mill. Anyone can find you a supplier. The question is who carries it when the metal is wrong.

The verification chain

Five gates. Material does not advance until the gate behind it is closed.

  1. Gate 01

    Mill qualification

    No mill quotes your job until it has been audited on site and approved: business and export licences verified, credit checked, the floor walked. We do not source against a marketplace listing.

    Answers: Who am I actually buying from?

  2. Gate 02

    Specification lock

    Grade, standard, dimensional tolerance and the full test regime are fixed in writing before a purchase order exists. The standard defines the tests, and we specify to it, so nothing is left to interpretation later.

    Answers: What exactly did I order?

  3. Gate 03

    Production surveillance

    Presence during the run, not a phone call at the end. Progress, heat numbers and photographs reach you while the material is still on the floor and still fixable.

    Answers: Is it being made the way we agreed?

  4. Gate 04

    Independent pre-shipment verification

    An accredited third-party laboratory tests the actual heat against the standard, and positive material identification is run on the delivered material. We put a representative on the floor at loading, with the authority to stop the container.

    We don’t ask you to trust our inspection — we’re not the ones inspecting. The lab has no stake in whether your shipment moves, which is exactly why its report is worth something. You receive it unedited, pass or fail.

    Answers: Is it the grade it says it is?

  5. Gate 05

    Landed accountability

    Freight, customs and delivery to your yard. An Australian entity, an AUD invoice, local recourse, and material replaced if it fails to meet what was specified.

    Answers: Who do I call when it’s wrong?

The questions we actually get asked

How much does verification add to the lead time?
Days, not weeks. Testing is arranged while the material is still at the mill, so it runs alongside production rather than after it. The only gate that costs real time is the one that catches a problem, and that is time you would otherwise spend discovering it in Australia.
Is there a minimum order?
No fixed minimum. Below a certain volume the freight economics stop working in your favour, and we will say so rather than take the order — for a small quantity an Australian distributor is sometimes genuinely the better answer.
What happens if the material fails the test?
It does not ship. You receive the report showing why, and we go back to the mill or to another approved mill. You are never asked to accept off-spec material at a discount.
Can we nominate the inspection house?
Yes. If you already work with an accredited laboratory, we will use them. It costs us nothing and it removes any question about whose interests the report serves.
Do you hold stock in Australia?
No. Everything is sourced to order against your specification, which is what allows the grade, the heat and the test regime to be fixed in writing before anything is made.
What are your payment terms?
Set out before you commit, and they depend on the engagement. Under Managed Sourcing you contract and pay the mill directly. Under Delivered you are buying from an Australian company on AUD terms.
